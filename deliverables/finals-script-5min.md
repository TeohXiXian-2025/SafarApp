### 1 · The story (0:00–0:55)

*Press Enter to start, then → once per scene after it settles. The bold lines match the captions and headlines on screen. Turn captions off with C if you'd rather the judges only hear you.*

> *Scene 1:* "**Imagine finally going on your dream holiday with your closest friends.** Aminah, Jun, Mei and Daniel. Four friends, one dream trip to Japan."

> *Scene 2:* "**Everyone wants to explore local cuisine. But finding a suitable dining option isn't always straightforward.** Aminah asks: is this place halal? The answer: **halal status unavailable.** The nearest option is **18 minutes away.**"

> *Scene 3:* "**And for Muslim travellers, prayer is an essential part of the journey.** In Kyoto, Aminah gets the reminder: **prayer time approaching.** Where can she pray nearby? And Daniel sees that **Kiyomizu will run late.**"

> *Scene 4:* "**Different apps, fragmented information and endless group coordination.** Four different apps. One very complicated trip."

> *Scene 5:* "**The problem isn't that we travel differently. It's that our planning tools don't bring our differences together.**"

> *Scene 6:* "**What if we could bring everyone's needs together in one seamless travel experience?**"

### 2 · Safar (0:55–1:10)

> "That's Safar, **the Muslim Travel OS**: **prayer-aware planning, halal discovery and group decisions in one workspace.** We're **Team Ds Gang.**"

### 3 · The market (1:10–1:30)

*→ reveals each figure.*

> "There were **196 million international Muslim arrivals in 2025**, and **annual Muslim travel spending** is projected to reach **310 billion dollars by 2030**. And it's not only pilgrimage. It's **faith journeys and everyday holidays**: **food, culture and meaningful travel.**"

### 4 · How travellers plan today (1:30–1:50)

*→ reveals the tools, the gap, then Safar.*

> "Today, people use good tools that each do one job. **Wanderlog** for **shared itineraries, maps and reservations.** **HalalTrip** for **halal discovery, mosques and prayer tools.** **WhatsApp and Sheets** for the conversation and **a manually maintained schedule.** No single tool connects **food evidence, prayer windows and a group decision.** Safar is **a shared itinerary that carries member requirements into planning decisions.**"

### 5 · The problem (1:50–2:10)

*→ reveals each problem, then the community posts.*

> "Our research kept showing three problems. **Prayer blind spots**: a prayer time **also needs a nearby place and room in the itinerary.** **Scattered halal checks**: **maps, menus and social posts each hold only part of the food evidence.** And **mixed-group friction**: **a majority vote can leave a strict need unresolved and companions waiting.**"

### 6 · Our answer (2:10–2:30)

*→ reveals each answer.*

> "So **every pain has a product response.** **Dynamic prayer anchoring** makes prayer windows and nearby facilities part of the shared day. A **location-based Halal Radar** shows each nearby option with its evidence. And **AI compromise and split planning** suggests a middle ground, or parallel activities **with a regroup point.**"

### 7 · Two paths (2:30–2:50)

*→ reveals each path, then the regroup bar.*

> "Think back to Aminah and Jun. **One app, two paths.** Aminah, the **Muslim member**, sets her **halal requirements and prayer preferences**, then visits **a nearby prayer place and checks wudu facilities.** Jun, the **non-Muslim companion**, sees **the same itinerary and the planned prayer break**, and **chooses a nearby café.** Then they **regroup at an agreed place and time.**"

### 8 · The workspace (2:50–3:00)

> "It all lives in **the Safar travel workspace**, where **the itinerary, map and group context stay together**: **shared itinerary, prayer context, map and weather, and group decisions.**"

### 9 · Three more tools (3:00–3:20)

*→ reveals each tool.*

> "And **three tools for the rest of the journey.** The **Social Spot Extractor** turns a supported social link into places to review. The **AI Document Vault** checks **passport dates and booking details**, and spots **missing hotel nights.** **Transit and Disruption Support** watches flights and weather, and proposes **a schedule resync.**"

### 10 · Launch (3:20–3:40)

*→ reveals the audience, then revenue.*

> "We'll launch in **Malaysia**, with **mixed university and corporate travel groups**: **Muslim and non-Muslim members travelling together.** We start with **Tokyo and Kyoto**, then **Istanbul**, and later **Makkah and Madinah.** This is **a business model to validate**: **Freemium and Pro** plans, **travel-agency APIs**, and **halal dining referrals.**"

### 11 · How it's built (3:40–4:05)

*→ reveals each layer, the loop, then security.*

> "**One request. Multiple intelligent services. One seamless journey.** The **traveller experience** is a **React 19 web app**, with the **prayer engine calculating prayer times locally.** One **Vercel API router** handles **planning rules and guard rails**, with **Firebase** to **sync, store and secure** the trip. It calls **Gemini, with Groq as a fallback**, plus **Google Places and OpenStreetMap** to **find halal with ease.** The result is a **personalised travel experience**, with **security by design**: **secret API keys remain server-side.**"

### 12 · Safar in action (4:05–4:45)

*Switch to the demo tab, or press B for the screenshots if the connection is weak.*

> "Let's see **one Japan trip, from a saved idea to a coordinated day.** First, **social discovery**: we **review candidate places from a supported link.** Then **prayer and food**: we **inspect a prayer window and a food evidence label.** Next, **group compromise**: the **Ichiran conflict, an alternative and a regroup.** Finally, a **document check**: **booking fields and a hotel coverage gap.**"

### 13 · Close (4:45–5:00)

> "Aminah, Jun, Mei and Daniel shouldn't have to choose between their friends and their needs. With Safar, it's **a shared trip where prayer, food needs and group choices shape the plan together.** **Faith first. Group first. Travel better. Thank you. We are Team Ds Gang.**"

---

**Tips**

- Pause after "**halal status unavailable**" and after the scene 5 line. Those two pauses carry the opening.
- Every → reveal is noted in the cues. Say the line as its point appears, not before.
- If you're running long, cut slide 8 to its first sentence and drop the destinations on slide 10.
- Practise once with a timer. Slide 12 is the easiest place to lose time.
