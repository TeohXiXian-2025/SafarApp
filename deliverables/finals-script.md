## Spoken script

### 01 · Safar · The Muslim Travel OS

**0:00–0:25**

> Good day, judges. We are Team Ds Gang, and this is Safar: the Muslim Travel OS. We bring prayer-aware scheduling, halal discovery and mixed-group decisions into one shared travel workspace. Our goal is simple: help friends travel together while respecting what each person needs from the trip.

**Cue:** Introduce the product, then the team. “Travel OS” describes the shared workspace, not a device operating system.

### 02 · A large market with specific travel needs

**0:25–1:10**

> The original idea starts with an audience whose travel needs affect every day of a journey. The Mastercard–CrescentRating Global Muslim Travel Index reports 196 million international Muslim arrivals in 2025 and projects annual spending of 310 billion dollars by 2030. That includes a much wider opportunity than pilgrimage alone. Hajj and Umrah are important journeys, alongside family holidays, city breaks and trips with non-Muslim friends. Across these situations, food requirements and daily prayer influence where people go, when they move and how the group stays together. That is the planning context Safar is built around.

**Cue:** Pause on the two figures. Call the spending figure a projection. These figures replace the prototype’s older, unsourced estimates while preserving its market argument.

**Source:** [Official source](https://crescentrating.com/insights/gmti)

### 03 · Muslim travel planning is fragmented

**1:10–2:00**

> The prototype research surfaced three connected problems. First, a prayer time is easy to look up, but the group still needs a suitable place and enough room in the schedule. Second, checking a meal can mean switching between maps, menus, social posts and halal directories. Third, mixed groups need different things at the same moment. The original community screenshots show people describing that friction: checking several apps, struggling with food choices and negotiating prayer breaks with friends. These are qualitative examples. They explain our design direction. Imagine reaching a ramen restaurant and discovering the broth conflicts with a member’s requirements. The group now has to solve food, timing and regrouping together.

**Cue:** Point to the three original community screenshots. Click any screenshot to enlarge it. Do not read the hostile wording aloud or present the posts as a representative survey.

### 04 · How travellers plan today

**2:00–2:45**

> Travellers already have useful tools. They discover places through TikTok, Instagram, Xiaohongshu and Google Maps. Wanderlog supports shared itineraries, maps and reservations. HalalTrip provides halal discovery and prayer tools. Many groups also coordinate in WhatsApp and maintain a spreadsheet. Our opportunity is the connection between these jobs. When a food choice changes, the group also needs to reconsider prayer, travel time and where everyone meets again. Safar carries those requirements into the same decision and the same itinerary. That combination of faith needs and mixed-group coordination is the focus of our product.

**Cue:** Retain the original competitor context without repeating unverified “zero alternatives” claims. Explain Safar’s focus through a concrete change to the group’s itinerary.

**Source:** [Official source](https://wanderlog.com/plan-a-trip), [Official source](https://www.halaltrip.com/islamic-travel-app)

### 05 · Every pain has a product response

**2:45–3:25**

> Each of those problems maps directly to a Safar feature. Prayer blind spots become dynamic prayer anchoring: the window and nearby facilities are visible inside the day. Scattered food searches become location-based Halal Radar, with the available evidence beside each option. Conflicting choices become compromise and split planning. The group can choose a middle ground, or take a short separate activity and meet again. The image captures the situation we want to improve: one person anxiously checks a menu while a companion waits. Safar gives both people a plan they can review before that moment arrives.

**Cue:** Follow each problem directly into its named feature. The image is illustrative, not a photograph from a field study.

### 06 · One app. Two paths. A shared regroup.

**3:25–4:15**

> This is the two-path journey at the heart of the prototype. Both travellers join the same trip. The Muslim member records halal and prayer requirements. The non-Muslim companion records their own food and activity interests. Everyone can see the shared itinerary. At a prayer break, one path can lead to a nearby mosque or prayer room, with wudu information where available. The other can include a café or a short activity nearby. Both paths end at an agreed regroup point. Mentor feedback helped us recognise why this matters: a mixed group needs more than majority voting. It needs a way for different requirements to coexist inside one trip.

**Cue:** Trace the shared start, then the two columns, then the shared regroup. The café is an example of a reviewed parallel activity, not a guaranteed automatic assignment.

### 07 · The Safar travel workspace

**4:15–4:50**

Weather adds context for outdoor activities, while shared updates let members see changes. The current application carries that workflow through People, Bookings, Ideas, Food and Plan. Members contribute their needs and suggestions, then the trip lead reviews the result. We have kept the original workspace image here to show the product vision. In the demo, you will see the current working screens.

**Cue:** Point at the itinerary, prayer context, map and food warning. Clearly identify this as the original prototype workspace, then transition to current capabilities.

### 08 · Context-aware, prayer-first planning

**4:50–5:45**

> Three capabilities make this work. First, local prayer calculations anchor the timeline to the destination and date. The planner considers these windows alongside fixed bookings, meals and travel buffers. Second, Halal Radar makes evidence visible. Certification evidence, Muslim-owned claims and pork-free labels are different signals. Listed, likely or unchecked results show what still needs confirmation. Walking distance and recent queue reports add context where available. Third, dietary conflict detection connects a warning to a decision. In the current Ichiran example, the app surfaces the food conflict and offers middle-ground options for members who are not going. A reviewed split keeps the regroup plan visible. Auto-plan then lets the trip lead inspect the resulting schedule before applying it.

**Cue:** Use the current Ichiran screenshot as evidence. Say “local calculations,” not “live prayer API.” Source labels do not replace venue confirmation for strict requirements.

### 09 · Three tools for the rest of the journey

**5:45–6:40**

> The prototype also included three tools for the messy parts of travel, and they remain part of Safar. The Social Spot Extractor turns a supported TikTok, Instagram or other accessible link into candidate places for review. The Document Vault checks extracted passport and visa fields. Booking review reads tickets and hotels, while stay coverage can reveal an uncovered night. For example, a checkout one day before the flight should prompt the group to review that gap. Travellers confirm extracted fields and official document requirements themselves. Finally, configured flight monitoring and weather alerts can raise a disruption report. The trip lead previews a schedule resync, so a delay can inform the itinerary. The app does not reissue airline or rail tickets.

**Cue:** Keep all three original supporting features. The visuals are labelled prototype concepts. Avoid implying universal social-link support, legal visa decisions or automatic ticket rebooking.

### 10 · Malaysia as our launch hub

**6:40–7:35**

> Our proposed launch hub is Malaysia. We would begin with friend and student groups planning outbound trips, where the food, prayer and mixed-group coordination problem is easy to observe. Tokyo and Kyoto are our first demonstration setting. The wider direction includes city trips to Istanbul and, later, faith-travel use cases around Makkah and Madinah. The original business model has three paths: a freemium product with paid group-planning features, API licensing for travel agencies, and halal dining referral commissions. These are hypotheses to validate. First we want to see whether groups finish planning with less rework, resolve conflicts more clearly and return for another trip. That evidence should guide what we charge for and where we expand.

**Cue:** Present Malaysia as the proposed launch hub, not an established user base. Preserve all three revenue paths without inventing prices, customers or partnerships.

## Factual updates from the prototype

The original story and feature coverage stay intact. These wording changes keep the pitch aligned with available evidence and the current application:

- Market scale uses the sourced GMTI 2026 figures in slide 2. The old 1.8B, $230B, 160M and 10.5M figures had no embedded sources and are not repeated as current facts.
- The original Reddit examples remain. The 72%, 91% and 45-minute statements are not presented as measured research because their source and method were not supplied.
- Wanderlog and HalalTrip keep their place in the comparison. Their official feature descriptions replace absolute claims that no other solution exists or that HalalTrip has no prayer tools.
- Prayer calculations use the adhan library locally. Food labels show available evidence. Member and trip-lead review remain part of decisions, extracted document fields and resync proposals.
- “AI compromise & split planning” retains the original Auto-Split concept while making the approval step explicit. Safar does not automatically certify restaurants, decide visa eligibility or reissue transport tickets.
- Malaysia launch, destination expansion and the three revenue paths remain proposals. No user traction, paid demand or partnerships are implied.

## Q&A preparation

**What makes Safar distinctive?** It brings prayer windows, food evidence and mixed-group requirements into a shared planning decision, including a reviewed alternative or split with a regroup plan.

**What is live?** The deployed application and guest demo support shared trips, preferences, bookings, ideas and votes, food discovery and prayer-aware planning. External AI, places, weather and flight functions depend on configuration, quotas and provider availability.

**How do halal labels work?** Safar exposes the source and uncertainty of the evidence. A listed or likely result is different from certification evidence. Travellers confirm strict requirements with the venue.

**What happens when people disagree?** The app surfaces the reason and presents reviewable options. The trip lead chooses whether to accept, reserve, reject or apply a proposed plan.

**How will the team validate the business?** Start with real Malaysian mixed groups. Observe planning rework, missed constraints, successful conflict resolution and repeat use, then test willingness to pay or partner demand.

**What did the team learn during ideation?** Mentor feedback expanded a Muslim-only planner into a mixed-group workspace and showed why simple majority voting does not resolve strict requirements.
